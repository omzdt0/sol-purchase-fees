## Maker fee, taker fee, and spread explained

The fee itself is only one part of the purchase cost.

A **taker order** consumes an existing order from the book. Market orders are typically taker orders because they seek immediate execution.

A **maker order** adds liquidity to the order book. A limit order may receive maker pricing if it sits on the book before being filled.

The basic spot fee calculation is:

text
Trading fee = fee rate × executed trade amount


For example, if a buyer purchases $1,000 worth of SOL and the applicable taker fee is 0.350%, the trading fee would be approximately $3.50 before considering any other cost.

That example is only an illustration. Your actual rate depends on the account tier and pair shown in the order panel.

The express Buy/Sell flow works differently. OKX explains that express purchases and Convert transactions generally show an all-in quote rather than a separate maker or taker fee. The difference between the buy and sell quote can include a spread.

This is why comparing only a headline “trading fee” can be misleading. A purchase with a low displayed commission may still be more expensive if the quote has a wide spread.

Before buying SOL, compare:

- The quoted SOL price
- The amount of SOL you will receive
- The separate fee, if shown
- The estimated withdrawal fee
- Any payment processing charge
- Whether the quote expires before you confirm
